  <p class="article-deck">How should a 100,000-person lunar colony receive 100 million metric tons of construction material? Our model compares a space elevator, reusable ground rockets, and a dynamic hybrid strategy that changes as lunar resource production matures.</p>

  <figure class="article-hero">
    <img src="{{ '/images/projects/mcm/optimization-process.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Differential Evolution search, convergence, and cost-time trade-off plots for the lunar logistics model">

  </figure>

  <h2>The modeling idea</h2>
  <p>The elevator is inexpensive per kilogram but initially bottlenecked by immature In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU). Ground rockets deliver cargo sooner and can later join the apex fleet, but relying on them throughout the mission is costly and carbon intensive. We therefore let the allocation between both systems evolve over time.</p>

  <h2>Optimization and robustness</h2>
  <p>I led the mathematical modeling and programming. The final workflow combined nonlinear simulation, Differential Evolution, a 5,000-run Monte Carlo reliability study, and sensitivity analysis. The optimized hybrid policy shifts through rocket-dominant, transition, and elevator-dominant phases as ISRU matures.</p>
